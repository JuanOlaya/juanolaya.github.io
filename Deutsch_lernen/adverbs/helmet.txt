\section{User Study}

The user study was conducted to evaluate how effectively the \textit{TurnHelmet} communicates directional information through weight-shifting and whether this mechanism can encourage head movement consistent with shoulder checking. The study focused on three distinct movement patterns—\textit{rotation}, \textit{step}, and \textit{swing}—and assessed their effect on accuracy, response time, confidence, and perceived workload.

\subsection{Study Design}

A within-subject, counterbalanced design was employed to minimize learning and order effects. Each participant experienced all three movement patterns, with the sequence of conditions determined using a Latin Square design (3! = 6 possible orders). This ensured that every pattern appeared equally often in each position across participants.

Six participants (2 women, 3 men, 1 non-binary; aged 23–30) completed the study. All participants reported normal or corrected-to-normal vision and no neck or vestibular issues. Each session lasted approximately 30 minutes, including briefing, training, and data collection.  

Each participant completed three conditions corresponding to the three haptic movement patterns. For each condition, participants completed ten randomized trials—five with clockwise (right) rotation and five with counterclockwise (left) rotation.

\subsection{Measurements}

To evaluate both objective performance and subjective perception, the following metrics were collected:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Accuracy:} The proportion of correctly identified movement directions.
  \item \textbf{Response time:} The duration between the onset of motion and the participant’s response.
  \item \textbf{Confidence:} Self-reported certainty in the perceived direction on a 7-point Likert scale.
  \item \textbf{Mental and Physical Demand:} Subscales from the NASA TLX questionnaire were used to assess cognitive effort and physical strain after each condition.
\end{itemize}

In addition, two open-ended qualitative questions were asked after each condition:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item What helped you to perceive the direction?
  \item What hindered you from perceiving the direction?
\end{enumerate}

At the end of the session, participants were also invited to provide improvement suggestions.

\subsection{Tasks and Procedure}

At the beginning of each session, participants read and signed an informed consent form. They were introduced to the \textit{TurnHelmet}, its purpose, and safety precautions. Before the main trials, participants completed a short training phase where they experienced each of the three haptic patterns once. During this familiarization, participants were informed whether their directional judgments were correct to ensure comprehension of the task.

In the main study, each condition followed the same procedure. Participants sat upright in front of a computer while wearing the \textit{TurnHelmet}. They placed their hands on a keyboard and were instructed to identify the perceived rotation direction by pressing the left or right arrow key. Each trial began with the experimenter triggering the movement; the rotation continued until the participant responded. Immediately afterward, participants rated their confidence level.

After each block of ten trials, participants completed the NASA TLX subscales (mental and physical demand) and answered the two qualitative questions. Short breaks were offered between conditions to prevent fatigue or discomfort.

This controlled laboratory setup allowed for consistent measurement of how effectively the three haptic patterns supported directional perception and physical head movement.
